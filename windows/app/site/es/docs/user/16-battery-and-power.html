Battery and Power

The battery, power level and power switch values depend on the radio. The Radio reference has them for each radio. A radio without a battery (a mobile on a fixed supply) has none of the battery items in this chapter: no battery icon or percentage, no Tools > Battery page, no Battery saver or Low battery warning setting, and no battery warnings.

Battery level

The status line shows the battery as an icon and a percentage. On a small screen a page's title takes over the status line, so the battery shows on Home and on the larger screens' pages. The percentage turns to the warning color at the low battery warning level and below. The level is set in Settings > Power saving > Low battery warning (15 % by default).

OK, 6, 9 opens Tools > Battery, which shows the level. At the warning level and below it also shows "charge soon".

The radio reads the battery once a second. It doesn't read it while transmitting or for 3 s after, because the voltage sags under transmit.

Low and critical battery

At the low battery warning level the radio shows the banner "Battery 15%: charge soon" (with the actual percentage) and plays the low battery beep. The warning repeats every 5 minutes while the battery stays at or below that level. It stops once the battery is 3 % above the level (charged).

Most radios also have two voltage thresholds. Whichever warning comes first applies.

Level What the radio does
Low Low battery beep (800 Hz, then 600 Hz), again every 2 minutes. The backlight is limited to 30 %
Critical Critical beep (600 Hz three times), again every 30 s. Every transmission is refused

Three readings in a row below a threshold change the level. The level goes back up when the battery is 0.2 V above the threshold. A radio without voltage thresholds has the percentage warning only.

Transmit power

Each radio has its own set of power levels. Each channel's power comes from the codeplug. A channel without one uses the radio's default level.

Way to change it Levels
*, 1 Three steps: the radio's lowest level, its default, its highest
Power level key on Home All of the radio's levels, one per press (radios with that key only)

Both changes are temporary. Hold OK to save the level to the channel.

Power levels use the radio's factory calibration. Use the lowest power that reaches the other station.

Backlight

Settings > Display > Brightness sets the backlight level. Quick settings has it too (*, 9). On radios that switch backlight speed to keep the audio quiet, a dimmed screen brightens while transmitting or receiving audio and returns to its set level when idle.

Settings > Display > Backlight Dim/Off sets two times: when the backlight dims to a fifth of the brightness, and when it goes off. 30s/2m dims after 30 s and goes off after 2 minutes. 30s/dim dims after 30 s and stays on, readable at a glance (on a desk or a bench), at the cost of some battery. Any key brings it back.

The backlight also comes on for what needs you:

Comes on Stays as it is
A call you hear (DMR), or a signal that opens the squelch (FM) A DMR call on a talkgroup you don't listen to
A message, call alert, private call or emergency The radio's own data: ARS registration, ACKs, location answers
Your own transmission, or a refused PTT Timed beacons (APRS), roaming probes, hotspot traffic
Broadcast FM that keeps playing

Battery saver

Settings > Power saving > Battery saver puts the receiver to sleep on an idle FM channel. After 10 s without activity the receiver sleeps and wakes for 100 ms at a time to listen. Any signal in a listening window wakes it fully. It is off by default.

These stop the battery saver:

  • a key press, monitor, scan or VOX;
  • a DMR channel, or hotspot mode;
  • a channel the codeplug marks "no battery saving".

Auto power off

Settings > Power saving > Auto power off switches the radio off after a time without use: Off, 30 min, 1 h or 2 h (Off by default). A key press, a call received or sent, a scan and hotspot mode count as use. One minute before, the radio beeps and shows "Switching off in 1 min / Any key keeps it on". Any key cancels it.

On a radio with the power switch on its volume knob, the knob is still on when auto power off acts. Whether each radio can cut its own power then is in the Radio reference. A radio that can't cut it comes back up after 2 s, shows "Couldn't switch off / Turn the knob off", and keeps running as before.

Turning off

Turn the radio off with its power switch. On the handhelds this is the volume knob, turned counterclockwise until it clicks. Where the radio sees the switch, it finishes what it is doing (an ARS deregistration, at most 3 s), saves its settings and switches off. The Radio reference describes the power switch of each radio.
