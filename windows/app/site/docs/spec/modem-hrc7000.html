HR_C7000 as a DMR modem

How the baseband inside the HR_C7000 SoC is driven as a DMR Tier II layer-2 modem and as an FM modem: registers, buffers, interrupts, what to write when, and what the chip does by itself. Board-level facts (pins, RF chip, PA timer, calibration source) are on the board's hardware page (feature-matrix.md lists the boards); the stack-side contract is in dmr-modem.md; the HR_C6000 equivalent is in modem-hrc6000.md.

The modem runs DMR at layer 2: the CPU builds every burst's information bits (LC, CSBK, data blocks, AMBE) and the chip adds the FEC (BPTC, Golay slot type, RS(12,9), QR EMB, CRC with masks), sync, CACH handling and timeslot timing. Layer 3 (the chip's own call control, SMS, BS activation) exists but is not used by any working firmware.

AF receive input (datasheet, document 2). In AF mode the RF chip's demodulated audio goes to the AF ADC's P input, and a DAC output biases its N input; on some boards that is a board DAC output. Document 1 says the AF bias is set through SIG_CENTER instead. A DC step on either input moves the PHASE_OUT center, which the DMR receiver reads as a frequency offset. AF mode has no frequency offset compensation: RX_IF_FREQ applies to IF mode only.

Reference oscillator control (MOD1, datasheet). In receive the MOD1 level is RF_MOD_BIAS_CTRL b25:16 over 0-3.3 V, which holds only while DAC_CONTROL keeps that DAC powered. In TX it is SIG_CENTER b31:24, ±422 mV around 1.65 V in 3.3 mV steps; RF_MODE b9 sets where the ramp starts (0 = 1.65 V). With cal + 384 in b25:16 both states give about the same voltage, within one step (measured).

2.1a RF_MODE bit 11: the DCDM receive switch

The HR_C7000 datasheet names bit 11 use_dual_phy. It turns on a second receive channel, "for repeater chip receive mode and DCDM mode" (datasheets/C7000/HR_C7000_Document_2.pdf, register table at p. 98; our translation from the Chinese). DCDM is dual-timeslot direct mode: two conversations on the two DMR timeslots of one frequency, with no repeater to supply sync. The community name is "dual-capacity direct mode"; the datasheet doesn't expand it. Its DCDM set-up (section 18.3.5.1, p. 410) is three steps: turn off MS and BS sync receive, enable TDMA1 and TDMA2 sync detection, and set RF_MODE[11]. Receive stays enabled all the time.

  • Channel mode. The bit can follow the channel mode: simplex channels (RX frequency equal to TX frequency) set it and enable TDMA1 and TDMA2 sync; repeater channels clear it and enable only MS and BS sync. That is the datasheet's DCDM set-up on simplex. The datasheet also lists "repeater chip receive mode" as a user of bit 11, which a repeater channel with the bit clear suggests is a different mode. Not resolved.
  • RadioFoundry: writes 0x03269860 (bit 11 set) and enables the TDMA1 and TDMA2 sync detection on every channel (hrc7000.c, the bench note of 2026-10-16), so RadioFoundry runs the DCDM receive set-up on repeater channels too. Whether that matters on a repeater is not measured.
  • Not a second RF chip. The RF chip is unchanged. The second channel is a second modem receive path. Multi-chip dual watch is a separate feature (RadioFoundry issue #96).
  • Not known: whether a simplex receiver with bit 11 on hears a second-timeslot call that it misses with the bit off. No on-air test has compared the two (section 10, item 7).

Voice syncs are not programmed; the chip derives them (they are the bitwise complements of the data syncs; unverified). Bit 31 of the RECV_MS / RECV_BS high halves is an undocumented enable. The RECV_TDMA1 / RECV_TDMA2 high halves need it too: without it the chip never detects the direct mode syncs (bench, 2026-10-16: an SFR's TS1 inbound was not heard until it was set).

SEND_DATA_SYNC is the only send pattern; a voice burst goes out with its complement. RadioFoundry rewrites it before a burst that asks for another sync family (dmr_tx_burst_t.sync_override: an SFR's TS2 direct mode outbound) and back to MS for the next burst without one. On air the burst then carries that family (bench, 2026-10-16: TS2 sync with the repeated LC).

Values written to LAYER2_CONTROL: 0xC0 = TX and RX enabled, passive timing (receive, follow the other side's slots); 0xC3 = TX and RX enabled, active TX and RX timing (we set the slot clock); 0xCB = 0xC3 plus b3 rx_master_auto_update, for tx_listen. The datasheet (10.2.2.16) says b0 rx_master_mode should normally be 0, and that in active receive mode the receive slot edges move with the received sync only when b3 is set. With 0xC3 alone an SFR heard almost nothing in its listening slot while it sent (measured, 2026-10-16: L18/1), so tx_listen sets b3 for a transmission on our own timing.

An aligned start (DMR_TIMING_ALIGNED with a burst received, an SFR's repeat) writes no LAYER2_CONTROL value: it stays 0xC0 and LAYER2_SLOTON = 0x25 (0x05 plus b5 dll_rx_open_tx) builds the TX slot from the receive slot. Any LAYER2_CONTROL write at the start left the receiver without slot sync for about 1 s, deaf to the call being repeated: 0xC3 (TX slots 37.7-41.6 ms after the caller's instead of 30), 0xC4 (b2, TX slots following the receiver: 28.6-30.1 ms), even 0xC4 written over 0xC4. With b0 rx_master_mode set (0xC5, 0xCD) the receiver kept its sync but no TX slot started. Without a CACH (an SFR's repeat, a direct-mode answer) the first slot after the start is the one the received bursts came in; the driver sends in the slot whose number (LAYER2_STATUS b31) is the received bursts' chip slot for the same TS, the other one for another TS. Measured on the SDR bench, 2026-10-16: our bursts 30.1 ms after the caller's, the whole call heard and repeated.

Through a repeater (BS-sourced bursts; two boards on the SDR bench, issue 72, 6 of 6 runs on the right slot and on the grid) the slot comes from the CACH TC alone (5.3): the TC lock, then a TC estimate that toggles at every timeslot interrupt and gives the TC of the slot starting at that interrupt. The receiver runs in LAYER2_CONTROL 0xC4 (TX slots follow the receiver) from every receive restart on a repeater channel, and that restart releases both slot syncs (TXRX b23 and b22) and is also the end of every transmission. A start waits in receive for the lock, then writes only TXRX (no LAYER2_CONTROL or SLOTON write at the key-up); the first TX tick sends in the next slot when the estimate is the other TS's TC. A first TX-vector interrupt less than half a slot after the last receive one is the same slot and does not toggle the estimate. Without the lock in 1 s the start is refused (TX_REJECTED, nothing sent).

The same lock numbers the slot ticks on a BS signal: once it holds, each DMR_EV_SLOT_TICK carries the TS that the estimate gives for the slot starting there, and the AT bit (RX_TYPE_INFO b23) of the last CACH that named that slot, when it was read in the last four ticks (DMR_TICK_AT_KNOWN, DMR_TICK_AT_BUSY). Before the lock, while transmitting and without a CACH, ts is 0 and AT unknown. On a BS signal on both slots nearly every tick after the lock carries AT.

Timing rules the chip follows (what the bench showed; the data sheet doesn't say):

  • The TX slot phase is latched, not derived. In 0xC4 the TX slots follow the receiver, but only from the moment the TX slot sync was last released and re-acquired. A TX sync left from an earlier transmission, including a burst sent on our own timing (the wake-up), keeps its phase and the next call goes out off the repeater's grid by whatever the difference was. Every receive restart must release b22 as well as b23.
  • Check the phase, not the slot. The measure of "on the grid" is the first TX-vector interrupt 30 ms (one slot) after the last receive one (txh u in the diagnostics: 299-300 in the passing runs, 90-180 in the failing ones). It is cheaper to read than the air capture and gives the same verdict.
  • LAYER2_STATUS b31 is not a usable slot number at the start of a transmission (always 0), and RX_TYPE_INFO b19 is one burst's chip slot, not a reference. Neither decides a TX slot on a repeater.
  • Writing LAYER2_CONTROL drops the receiver's slot sync for about 1 s, in every value tried. Set it at a receive restart and leave it.
  • A TX-vector tick right after an RX-vector one is the same slot. The two interrupts of one slot came about 5 ms apart in some runs; counting both as slots put the estimate one slot off.
  • A deframe near a tick belongs to the previous slot. A burst is deframed late in its slot, around the next timeslot interrupt: in the first half of a slot count it to the one before.
  • A slot estimate needs a lock first. The first bursts after the receiver syncs can carry a wrong TC; the driver waits for four alternating values.

SFR (two boards on the SDR bench, 2026-10-18, each run after a key-up on our own timing): the repeat's TX slot comes 30.1 ms and 30.7 ms after the inbound one, with the TX slot sync released only on repeater channels (6 runs) and on every channel (10 runs) alike: the SLOTON b5 start takes the receiver's phase whatever the TX sync held, so the stale phase that broke the repeater path does not reach an SFR. Every receive restart releases both syncs. Read at an SFR's start, LAYER2_STATUS b31 picked the right slot in every run (txh e1: b31 set, the received chip slot next, ours after it). The first TX-vector interrupt comes two slots after the last receive one (txh u 599-600), not one as through a repeater: the slot right after the SLOTON strobe has no TX tick, so a tick count across an SFR's start is one short. The phase check is a whole number of slots. A direct mode start can take the slot from the TDMA1 / TDMA2 sync type in RX_TYPE_INFO b3:2 plus LAYER2_STATUS b29 against an offset captured at that moment, never from b31 or b19.

Values written to LAYER2_TXRX_CTRL: 0x00 nothing next slot; 0x40 receive next slot; 0x80 transmit next slot; 0x8040 receive next slot with the voice-access flag; 0x800000 release RX slot sync.

Embedded LC after late entry also appears at 0x00–0x08; the chip keeps the previous call's LC there until a new one is decoded (measured). 0x2B–0x2F: the CACH short LC of a BS signal, 28 bits and the CRC-8 received, the low 4 bits of 0x2F unused; new at system interrupt bit 4. The interrupt only counts it, and only while a BS signal is heard (the RAM keeps the last one); the event task reads it (hrc7000_dmr_short_lc, in flash: a new one comes every 120 ms at most) and reports it when it changes (DMR_EV_SHORT_LC); the stack checks the CRC-8. The chip's own CRC-8 flag (RX_TYPE_INFO b30, in the burst report) isn't used.

The driver saves each late-entry LC under the chip slot hrc7000_dmr_decode reads from RX_TYPE_INFO at the late-entry interrupt (current_rx_slot, b19) and attaches it only to a voice burst on that chip slot. A single pending LC can cross slots even when the voice burst filter works: an unselected slot's late-entry interrupt sets it, its deframe is dropped, and the next selected voice burst takes the wrong caller (#495, host reproduction). Separate pending buffers also preserve both LCs when both reports arrive before voice. A slot's pending LC is discarded by any non-voice burst on that slot (LC header, terminator, CSBK, MBC, data header, PI header, idle; a header with a bad CRC too), by a filtered burst, and when the slot's voice ages out (390 ms without a voice burst), so a call that fades before a voice burst takes its LC doesn't name the next headerless caller. Receiver resets and a slot filter change discard both. A failed late-entry checksum invalidates only the reporting slot's pending LC.

Nothing here confirms that b19 at a late-entry interrupt with no deframe names the burst that completed the LC rather than the last burst deframed; that needs on-air confirmation with calls on both slots. It does not infer a DMR timeslot from the chip slot: BS bursts retain their CACH TC labels.

Voice RAM (0x16000960): write 27 bytes before arming a voice burst; read 27 bytes after a voice deframe interrupt Burst numbering for privacy keystream purposes is RX_TYPE_INFO's 1–6.

3.1a The per-burst RF task

Every HR_C7000 board keys its RF per burst: the RF chip to the TX frequency and its TX state, the paths and supplies, the PA, and back to receive after the burst, so the radio hears the other slot (and a hotspot listens while keyed). Where the modem's MOD1 output modulates the reference oscillator instead of the RF chip, this is the analog transmitter's whole key-up, a few milliseconds with waits in it. The sequences run in a task of their own at the highest priority (platform/hrc7000/dmr_rfburst.c); the interrupts only wake it. The board gives the two sequences (hrc_dmr_board_t.burst, an hrc_rfburst_board_t) and arms and disarms the task from tx(), tx_off() and rx(); the glue starts the task and passes it every switch. A board's sequences are only its chip steps; the timing is the task's (dmr_rfburst_timing.h): a sequence's at waits count from the RF TX interrupt (a two-point key-up's RF switch and PA times are hrc_rfburst_switch_at_us and hrc_rfburst_pa_at_us), and the task holds the TX off until the burst's last symbol from the modem's rf_tx_lead. So a board whose switch must come late (to keep receiving the inbound burst before ours) sets a lead about 0.5 ms longer than that point and waits: the task can wake up to 0.42 ms after the interrupt.

A split duplex hotspot's base-station outbound (hrc7000_bs.c) is continuous: the task runs the TX-on sequence at its first RF TX interrupt and the TX-off sequence at once when it stops, with no hold and no RF RX interrupt ending it.

5.2 Start / restart receive

Used at DMR entry, after TX through a repeater (direct: 5.2a), after a stalled transmission, at a new DMR frequency (the radio core calls rx_restart then, gotchas.md 17.2) and wherever reception must be (re)acquired:

# Register Value Why
1 (enter DMR, 5.1)
2 VOICE_EMB_CTRL 0x02 burst F EMB from the chip's encoder
3 LAYER2_CONTROL 0xC0 TX/RX enabled, passive timing
4 LAYER2_SLOTON 0x00 drop slot establishment
5 LAYER2_TXRX_CTRL 0x800000 release RX slot sync
6 LAYER2_TXRX_CTRL 0x40 receive in the next slot
7 LAYER2_SLOTON 0x05 dll_rx_slot_on and dll_tx_slot_on

A sequence of TXRX 0xC00000, 0, 0xC00000, 0x8040 with no SLOTON writes, or a busy wait in the color code probe, does not acquire sync reliably. Use this one everywhere.

A restart also forgets what was heard: the polite-access busy window (dmr-modem.md) starts again with the next burst taken. Polite access ages the last decoded activity by now_us, with a 360 ms window, independently of timeslot interrupts. Every usable burst refreshes the window, including one later dropped by the slot or CRC filter; unusable deframes do not. The own-color-code and all-activity policies retain their existing color-code checks.

Receive recovery uses wall time too, from both the timeslot handler and the task poll: more than 390 ms without another usable burst, 600 ms of stale activity without a usable burst, or 150 ms after a direct TX hold. A restart clears the pending recovery; subsequent slot interrupts, deframes or abnormal-slot reports schedule it again, and the 600 ms count starts with the first of them, not at the restart (after a long silence, a count from the restart would restart the receiver under the next call's first burst). A silent receiver is not restarted repeatedly. This permits recovery when timeslot interrupts stop but system interrupts continue. Host register-model tests cover stopped timeslot interrupts.

5.2a End of a direct transmission: the hold

The end of a transmission comes on the TX tick a slot after our terminator, about 25 ms into the next slot. A direct answer in the other timeslot, right after our terminator (30 ms after its start), is then already being received, and a 5.2 restart there makes the chip report it with BS sync and, on some radios, a header CRC error (gotchas.md 2.10). So a direct transmission ends without the restart:

# Register Value Why
1 LAYER2_CONTROL 0xC0 passive timing from our active one; the slot syncs stay where our transmission left them
2 LAYER2_TXRX_CTRL 0x40 receive the next slot

The software state is reset as at a restart (the polite busy window too). A burst taken holds the receiver as any reception does; with none, the 5.2 restart comes once 5 slots (150 ms) have passed with nothing taken, so a station on a timing of its own is taken after that. Through a repeater the restart stays at once: the next transmission needs a new TC lock (issue 72). Diagnostics: rs restarts/lost/stale h holds.

The RF has the same window. An answer in our own timeslot comes one frame later, 60 ms after the start of our terminator (a MOTOTRBO portable's link ACK to confirmed data), and a receive set-up by the board then (idle, synthesizer, receive) misses it. The radio core ends a DMR transmission through hal_radio_t.dmr_tx_end, which the glue implements (hrc_dmr_tx_end): when the last burst's switch put the RF back to receive, the board's tx_end (hrc_dmr_board_t) ends the transmission and leaves the RF as it is if it is on the last rx() settings (frequency, trim, awake). Otherwise, and on a board without tx_end, the core calls tx_off() (gotchas.md 3.14).

6. Transmit sequences

LOCAL_CC keeps the receive code during an aligned start's TC lock wait. Once locked, or at an unaligned start, the driver writes the channel's transmit code in bits 3:0 and keeps it through the final burst. After TX it restores the receive filter or scan code. A receive filter changed during TX takes effect when TX finishes.

All transmit actions happen in the timeslot interrupts: the CPU arms one slot at a time with TXRX_CTRL = 0x80 and leaves the other slot unused (0x00, or 0x40 / 0x8040 to listen: tx_listen, an SFR's inbound slot), giving one burst per 60 ms in its timeslot (datasheet figures 99–100). Every arming also starts the PA one-shot of a board without burst sequences (3.1a). The RF TX interrupt that follows keys the RF chip.

Alternating slots: in the inactive slot TXRX_CTRL = 0; in the active slot:

  1. For burst A (n = 0): rewrite the call LC (or talker alias LC) into TX RAM bytes 0–8 for the embedded LC.

6.4 Data and CSBK

Per burst, in an active slot: write the payload to TX RAM (10 bytes for CSBK / MBC header / data header: the chip appends the CRC-16 with the programmed mask because SEND_TYPE b8 = 0; 12 / 18 / 24 bytes for rate ½ / ¾ / 1 blocks, CRC-9 and CRC-32 computed by the CPU), SEND_TYPE = data type << 4, TXRX_CTRL = 0x80, start the PA one-shot. Inactive slot: TXRX_CTRL = 0 (or 0x40 to listen for a response). Sequence for a packet: data header (0x60), blocks (0x70/0x80/0xA0), optional terminator. CSBK signaling: optional preamble CSBKs then the function CSBK, all 0x30 (datasheet appendices B, C). Open RX afterwards to catch the response.

Rate 3/4 blocks don't decode. The chip's trellis encoder doesn't start each rate 3/4 block in state 0 (TS 102 361-1 B.2.2). A block re-encodes bit for bit from the CPU's 18 octets with start state 4 or 7, varying between blocks and tries, and sometimes one more info bit differs in the second constellation point. A receiver that starts its decoder in state 0, as the standard has it, takes the wrong path through the first tribits: octets 0-1 (a confirmed block's DBSN and CRC-9) come out wrong, and the packet fails. Rate 1/2 blocks, headers and CSBKs are sent exactly. SEND_TYPE b8 makes no difference, and the register map has no encoder reset. The glue sets hal_dmr_modem_t.tx_rate34_bad, so the core sends no rate 3/4 of its own: confirmed IP goes at rate 1/2 (dmr-data.md 12.9). Rate 3/4 blocks the radio relays (hotspot, repeater) keep their rate and have the same fault.

8.4 Prompt / beep FIFO

Set AUDIO_CONTROL b7, then while AUDIO_CONTROL b31 is 0 write 64 bytes (32 × 16-bit samples, low byte first, 8 kHz) to 0x16000980..0x160009BF; clear b7 when done (measured). High byte first (16 samples per push) sounds distorted. Flushing stale audio: fill with 0x7F bytes.

RadioFoundry plays all speaker PCM through this FIFO (platform/hrc7000/audio_pcm.c). The CPU-fed speaker buffer at 0x180000A0 (AUDIO_CONTROL b5, section 3) gave rough, choppy beeps on one board with every sample layout tried, with no queue underruns (measured).

The FIFO takes two pushes from empty (64 samples, 8 ms) before b31 sets (measured), less than one 20 ms PCM block. The blocks come at the rate the FIFO plays them, so a queue that pushes each block as it arrives has the FIFO nearly empty at every block edge. It ran dry there: the queue emptied with the FIFO taking more 151 times in about 120 blocks of beeps, and every tone broke up at 20 ms intervals, its pitch jumping and its level dipping by up to 40 dB at the microphone (measured). audio_pcm.c therefore pushes nothing after the queue is emptied (route on, gate edges) until two blocks are queued, so the queue stays a block ahead of the FIFO for one block (20 ms) of latency. With that hold the queue never ran out in the same beeps. A dedicated timer checks the FIFO every 2 ms independently of the audio task: a voice decoder can occupy that task longer than the FIFO's 8 ms capacity. On seeing room, the timer schedules a write at least 200 µs later; the interrupt does not busy-wait during that interval. Each write interrupt pushes at most one chunk after checking the queue generation, speaker route, TX gate, FIFO ownership and available space again. Stop/start, queue resets and FIFO ownership changes cancel a pending write. Three queue entries can be published, with a fourth reserved for the producer: variable decode time can finish a third block before either primed entry has drained. The occupancy check occurs after decoding, so space freed during decoding can be used. The un field of the pg diagnostics line (below) counts run-outs and stays 0.

The FIFO is two halves of one push (32 samples, 4 ms) each. b31 clears exactly every 4.00 ms, as the modem moves on to play the other half, and the next push sets it again at once (measured). Write the 64 bytes as byte writes: with halfword or word writes the FIFO counts one entry per bus access and plays nothing usable (measured).

A push in the first microseconds after b31 clears can land in the half being played. From then on every chunk plays one chunk early or late: the audio jumps at every 4 ms boundary, a phase step of 360° × f × 4 ms (none at multiples of 250 Hz, the most near 125 Hz plus multiples of 250 Hz), heard as a buzz of broadband clicks at 250 Hz under the tone, limited to the 4 kHz band. It lasts until a later push happens to line the halves up again. Whether a push fell there depended on how the audio task's 2 ms poll lined up with the modem's 4 ms, so the fault came and went for seconds at a time and between beeps. The un field stayed 0 and the chunks went in at exactly 8 kHz: nothing was lost, only reordered. On a swept tone, broken stretches had the energy just above the tone's frequency 5-25 dB below the tone; whole ones 39-55 dB below it (measured, test_speaker_sweep). Traces of the broken stretches showed pushes that found b31 set on entry and clear microseconds later, then wrote at once; in clean ones, almost none.

Every push therefore waits 200 µs after it sees b31 clear before writing (twice the shortest wait that was clean; pushes 0.1-3 ms after the edge played in order in 12 of 12 sweeps). Only a push that saw the bit clear writes, so the bit can only have cleared before that look. Speaker PCM (audio_pcm.c) uses the deferred timer write above. APRS (hrc_afsk.c) uses the blocking wrapper in platform/hrc7000/prompt_fifo.h; at 250 pushes a second that wait costs about 5 % of the CPU while AFSK is active. Every sweep recorded with the settling delay played whole, 8 of them; one more recording had half its energy above 4.3 kHz, which an 8 kHz stream can't make, and was the capture's fault (measured).

The prompt path also feeds the TX modulator: with AUDIO_CONTROL b7 set during a DMR transmission, the beep's audio rides on the 4FSK. On one board the talk permit tone overlapped the second and third voice LC headers, which then carried no decodable sync on the SDR bench and the bench modem took the call by late entry; with b7 held clear while transmitting, all three headers decoded in five runs (measured). Keep b7 clear during any transmission.

audio_pcm.c keeps it clear for every board on the platform with one gate. The DMR glue closes it as the engine starts to transmit (tx_begin, and the hotspot's bs_key); while it is closed, b7 stays clear and nothing is pushed to the FIFO. It opens at least 50 ms later, once hrc_dmr_tx_active() is false: checked by the audio task (at each PCM block, or every 10 ms while gated), on each speaker route change and on each hrc_audio_pcm_ring() call (the audio task only exists once a stream has run, and APRS sets b7 without one). On both edges the speaker queue is emptied, a block the audio task was filling at that moment included, so a beep queued during the transmission is dropped, not played late. Boards set b7 only through hrc_audio_pcm_ring(), which applies the gate. The last of the DMR glue's diagnostics lines, pg g<closings> l<closings that found speaker audio still pending> ra<checks that found b7 set while closed> un<speaker queue run-outs>, checks it: l, ra and un stay 0. Its counters reset with the DMR counters.

So the talk permit can't sound during DMR TX, and the boards set CAP_BEEP_NOT_ON_AIR: the radio core plays it in a short receive-only hold before the first burst (radio-features.md 4). On air with a hotspot parrot, the beep was heard before each call and the echoed calls carried no trace of it, with l0 ra0 (measured). On the SDR bench, in six overs with the permit played before keying, each over started with three voice LC headers that decoded and every burst was within 0.2 dB of the over's level (measured; test_dmr_tx_permit_before_key).

The FIFO itself is not flushed at the gate. Filling it with 0x7F bytes would (above); the permit hold leaves only silence in it at key-up.

APRS transmit uses the same path on purpose (platform/hrc7000/hrc_afsk.c): during an FM transmission, with b7 set and the microphone muted by the board, the FIFO's samples are the modulation. A timer tops the FIFO up every 2 ms; speaker PCM is held off the FIFO meanwhile (hrc_audio_pcm_hold), since its chunks would land between the tones. Bell 202 decoded off air from this path with a valid FCS (measured, aprs.md 13). The DTMF tone generator (8.3) needs no PCM, but its deviation field tops out near 1.5 kHz, short of the 3 kHz APRS wants.

The datasheet doesn't document this route. It names two FM transmit audio sources, selected by AUDIO_CONTROL b0 (datasheet 7.2.2.1): the codec ADC (0) or the TX RAM (1). As a source, the TX RAM is 0x16000030-0x1600042F, two 512-byte halves of 256 samples (32 ms at 8 kHz), and FM_ADDR_SW (0x56C) names the half in use (1 low, 2 high); SYS_INTERP b15 is the FM TX interrupt. The prompt FIFO is documented only as playback to the codec DAC (datasheet 7.3). LINEOUT_CTRL (0x088) b1 is line out 1 and b0 line out 2, the same PWM waveform on both (RadioFoundry sets 0x01). Two-point modulation: SIG_CENTER b31:24 biases MOD1 (the VC-TCXO) and b23:16 MOD2 (the VCO), 3.3 mV a step; RF_MOD_BIAS b7:0 and b15:8 are the MOD1 and MOD2 amplitudes; RF_MODE b10 sends a 40 Hz test square. The prompt FIFO path decodes on some boards but not on others, which take the mixer route below.

The FM transmit limiter (FM_DEV_COEF b23:16) caps the deviation of audio that enters through the codec ADC, the microphone and the mixer route below. The prompt FIFO's ring play is not capped by it: AFSK through ring play reached 5.1 kHz peak with the limiter at 4. Through the ADC path the ceiling scales with limiter x TX coefficient (measured, 25 kHz, Bell 202 tones):

Limiter TX coefficient Peak deviation
4 0x20 1.65 kHz (clipped at every level tried)
4 0x30 2.48 kHz (clipped)
8 0x20 3.18 kHz (clipped)
0x0F 0x40 4.18 kHz
0x0F 0x20 4.57 kHz, max 4.84 kHz (not clipped)

So a limiter of 4 holds speech to about 1.65 kHz at 25 kHz. A board sets fm_tx_limit for each bandwidth.

Where ring play doesn't reach the modulator on its own, the codec's digital mixer (datasheet 6.5.2.10-11) carries the DAC's audio into the ADC's record path, which is the FM transmit input. CR_MIX (codec 0x10: b7 mix_en, b6 load, b5:0 the MIX_n address) and DR_MIX (0x11) reach MIX_0..3. MIX_2 b0 (mix_rec) makes the ADC's output ADC + DAC, and MIX_3 b7:4 = 0xF zeroes the ADC's own input in that sum. hrc7000_codec_dac_to_tx sets MIX_3 and then MIX_2, clears them in the reverse order and ends with CR_MIX = 0. The codec mic mute (CR_ADC b7) must stay off on this route: it mutes the mix too. Through this route Bell 202 decoded off air with a valid FCS (measured, a board where ring play alone sent a bare carrier).

8.5 FM receive audio to the CPU

In FM (WORK_MODE 0x80) the modem copies its demodulated receive audio into the RX RAM as well as to the codec (the datasheet's "analog receive recording", on by default in FM):

Item Value
Buffer RX RAM 0x160004E0..0x160008DF: two halves of 512 bytes (256 samples), used in turn
Sample format 16-bit, high byte at the lower address
Rate 8 kHz: the halves alternate every 32.0 ms (measured over 95 changes)
Half to read FM_ADDR_SW (0x56C) b1:0: 1 = the first half, 2 = the second
Interrupt SYS_INTERP bit 14 (fm_rx_interp) as each half fills; its mask bit (SYS_INTERP_MASK b14) is off in the power-on mask
Input the modem's AF input. On a board with a separate FM RF chip that is its audio output, routed by a board GPIO

The audio is not squelched: it carries noise while no signal is present, so a demodulator can work below the squelch. Receiver noise reaches full scale (peak 32767) with the reset AF gain (measured).

RadioFoundry reads it in the system interrupt (platform/hrc7000/fm_rx_audio.c): the DMR glue owns that interrupt and calls an FM hook for bit 14, in FM too, and the hook copies each half into a 128 ms ring for the APRS demodulator.

10. Air interface provider

drivers/modem/hrc7000/hrc7000_air.c makes the modem a hal_air_t provider (hal/hal_air.h) and a receive tap (hal/hal_baseband.h) under CONFIG_AIR; the glue (platform/hrc7000/dmr_glue.c) exports them as hrc_air and hrc_baseband for a board's board.air and board.baseband. It receives through the soft modem on the FM receive copy and transmits through the physical layer. Fit: continuous profiles at 4800 symbols/s (2400 as AIR_FIT_RATE_DOUBLED), 2 or 4 levels; every fit carries AIR_FIT_UNTESTED unless the board sets hrc_dmr_board_t.air_verified, once its receive and transmit were checked on air (10.4; feature-matrix.md has which radios).

Item How
Ownership A tap or a session opens only in DMR with no DMR transmission and no FM receive hook (APRS) running. The glue halts the slot engine, and the session lives in the engine's event and TX queues (_Static_assert): no RAM of its own. The interrupts go to the session; the modem task polls it instead of the engine. At close the queues are emptied and the engine runs again
Interrupts System (bit 14 copy half, bit 12 PHY_RDY), both timeslot vectors, RF TX. Their handlers run from RAM (9 #15)
Mode changes Leaving DMR under a session ends any transmission (PA off, AIR_EV_TX_DONE) and stops receive; the next DMR entry sets the copy up again
Events From the interrupts through an 8-entry queue to the modem task, which calls the session's callback. TX_SPACE events in a row merge into one; the last entry is kept for TX_DONE

10.2 Transmit: the physical layer

The timeslot mode of 2.10 with every slot sent, so the carrier runs from the first slot to the last without a ramp between slots. One 36-byte TX RAM half is one 144-symbol chunk (30 ms at 4800 symbols/s).

  1. tx_begin (at least one chunk queued, the core has called the board's TX on): save RF_MOD_BIAS and write it scaled (10.3); TXRX 0, SLOTON 0, WORK_MODE 0x80 then 0x22; both halves idle (the profile's idle byte); LAYER2_CONTROL 0x82 (own TX timing); AUDIO_BUFFER_CLR 3. The copy stops for the transmission.
  2. The first RF TX interrupt keys the RF (the board's rf and pa_arm ops), once, held to the end. Until then, and for HRC7000_AIR_TX_LEAD_SLOTS (2) boundaries after it, each boundary sends an idle slot and the queue waits (no underrun counted): the carrier is up about 45 ms before it carries modulation, and a first chunk sent in that time was lost.
  3. Each slot boundary (the first of the timeslot or PHY_RDY interrupts in a slot; one more within 15 ms is the same slot) writes only the half the next slot reads, alternating, then TXRX 0x80. A half takes the oldest queued chunk, idle after a short last chunk; with none queued, idle and one AIR_EV_TX_UNDERRUN. The half's last byte (symbols 140-143) is written at the next boundary, first thing: the chip reads it after the boundary that rewrites its half, so written with the rest it went out with the chunk two slots later. The slot set up at a boundary is on air about 5 ms later: AIR_EV_TX_STARTED carries that time plus half a symbol.
  4. After the AIR_TX_LAST chunk (or tx_end with nothing queued) one idle slot, then at the next boundary: TXRX 0, RF off, RF_MOD_BIAS back, the copy set up again (or WORK_MODE 0x80 without a receiver), AIR_EV_TX_DONE.
  5. Watchdog (modem task): no slot boundary for 200 ms, or no RF TX interrupt 200 ms after tx_begin, ends the transmission as tx_abort does.

Writing both halves at each boundary corrupted the end of the slot on air; writing only the next one gave no errors (measured). WORK_MODE 0x02 (continuous) never keyed on the bench.

A P25 call from one HR_C7000 radio (lowest power, 4.5 s keyed, 26388 symbols), recorded on the SDR bench and compared with the symbols the radio queued (measured):

Driver Units decoded Symbol errors Errors at symbols 140-142 of a slot Soft EVM

10.3 Deviation

The modulation bytes of RF_MOD_BIAS (b15:8, b7:0), which the board writes for DMR at its TX on, are scaled by dev_outer_hz / 1820 and clamped to 1..255; b31:16 stay. 1820 Hz is the outer deviation the calibrated DMR bytes give, not DMR's nominal 1944 Hz: scaled by 1800 / 1944 they gave 1685 Hz (93.6 % of P25's 1800 Hz) measured from the SDR bench's I/Q, which leaves an MMDVM hotspot little room for its own offset. Deviation is linear in them, about 25 Hz per step through zero, and 0x118 and FM_DEV_COEF don't move it (measured on one HR_C7000 radio). Outside 100-6000 Hz the fit says AIR_FIT_DEV_APPROX. The pulse filter is the chip's own (raised cosine for DMR), so every shape but RRC 0.2 is AIR_FIT_SHAPE_APPROX.

11. Open questions

  1. LCSS bits on voice bursts B–E (2.6): the datasheet sets them, the driver does not. Does late entry work on other radios receiving this modem's transmissions?
  2. Whether the 0x370–0x37C, 0x168 and bit-31 sync-enable writes matter.
  3. RF_RECV_DELAY 0x36B0 vs the datasheet's 2000 for AF mode.
  4. The real timing of the TX timeslot interrupt relative to the slot boundary (section 3.1 is inferred).
  5. Data and CSBK transmission on the C7000 have only been checked against the datasheet and shared host tests, not on air.
  6. FM sub-audio: the C7000's own CTCSS/CDCSS engine (section 8) is not used on a board with a separate FM RF chip; the driver routes TX CTCSS to that chip instead.
  7. RF_MODE b11 (2.1a) on simplex receive: does a radio with the bit set hear a call on the second timeslot that it misses with the bit clear? No on-air test has compared the two. A test: a call on one timeslot while the radio receives the other, with b11 on and then off. Also: RadioFoundry sets b11 on repeater channels. Does that change repeater reception?
  8. BS mode (2.9): where the chip's CACH AT, TC and short LC come from (likely BS_CACH and a short LC written at every RDY_NEXT_SHORT_LC). The split duplex TX half doesn't need it: it uses the physical layer (2.10).
  9. A slot length trim for the physical layer mode (2.10 finding 13): LAYER2_MAX_CNT has none. Whether the trim works in the layer 2 timing (LAYER2_CONTROL b5 layer2slot), and any other register that does, is untested.
