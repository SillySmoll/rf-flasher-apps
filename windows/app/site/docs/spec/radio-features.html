Everyday radio features

Scan, the FM transmit extras, beeps and power management in the radio core. Code: core/radio/scan.c (the scan engine, pure), core/radio/beep.c (beep patterns and synthesis), core/radio/vibe.c (vibration patterns), core/radio/battery.c (battery levels, the backlight helper), all glued into core/radio/radio_sm.c; board hooks in hal/hal_radio.h (tx_tone, tx_dtmf, rx_sleep, mic_gain, ref_trim) and hal/hal_power.h (mic_level, vibrate). Build switches: docs/build-config.md. Debug link: the features view section and RADIO ops 20-24 (docs/spec/debuglink.md 3.7, 3.8). Host tests: tests/host/radioFeaturesTest.c; HIL tests: tests/hil/test_radio_features.py.

1. Settings

The radio core keeps one radio_settings_t (core/radio/radio.h). It loads it from the settings store at start-up (the rs_* fields, settings-store.md 3) and saves every change made with radio_set_settings(), so the settings screen hands the whole struct over on a change and reads it back with radio_get_settings(). The debug link's SETTING op goes through radio_set_settings() too, so its changes are saved.

Every caller reads the struct, changes its own fields and sets it again. The settings screen reads it afresh for each change, so a change made elsewhere (the debug link, a host, the radio core) is kept. Until the radio task has taken in the last radio_set_settings(), radio_get_settings() answers with those settings, not the ones in force. A run of quick changes therefore never reads back a value from before its own last change.

2. Scan (CONFIG_SCAN)

Requests: radio_req_scan(cmd) with stop, zone, VFO, dual, cancel, nuisance or toggle; radio_req_scan_vfo(lo, hi); radio_req_scan_dual(pos); radio_req_dual(ref, sel) (2.1). The view shows scan (off / zone / vfo / dual), scan_phase and scan_skipped; the tuned channel or frequency is the core view's as usual.

  • Zone scan visits the current zone's channels in order, from the one it started on, skipping empty positions and channels marked "zone skip" in the codeplug. FM, DMR, P25 and NXDN channels mix freely: each stop tunes the channel as a channel change would (P25, NXDN: the engine starts there). Left out: parked channels, P25 trunked channels, and NXDN channels at a rate the board can't receive (p25.md 7.1, nxdn.md 6.4).
  • VFO scan steps the VFO frequency by its step from lo to hi and wraps. Without a range it sweeps the board band the VFO frequency is in. Both ends must be in one band, at least one step apart.
  • Dual watch alternates between two channels, skip flags ignored (2.1). radio_req_scan_dual(pos) (the debug link's) watches the current channel and position pos of the current zone. The UI on top of it (A and B, the screens, the keys) is in dual-watch.md.
  • Priority (zone scan): the start channel again after every 2 others.

Each stop: 50 ms settle (readings from the previous frequency are ignored), then scan_dwell_ms listening. A signal is "active" when FM squelch opens (with the RX tone if the channel needs one) or DMR plays a call (dmr.state rx). A carrier that isn't active yet earns a probe: 350 ms on FM channels that need a tone (CTCSS detection takes 100 to 250 ms), 700 ms on DMR; other FM channels move on at once. On DMR a carrier is the noise reading at the squelch level or the signal strength at the DMR scale (level 1 above -112 dBm, 3 dB a level: -100 dBm at the default level; some receivers' noise reading doesn't follow a DMR signal, gotchas 17.3). Level 1 is also at least 6 dB above the floor: the lowest strength read past the settle on the last 8 stops (the first stop has none), since a strength reading that isn't calibrated can sit above the scale on an empty channel (gotchas 17.4). A carrier seen in the last 150 ms still counts (direct mode is on air 30 ms in every 60, and the noise reading drops between bursts), and once the modem delivers bursts with the channel's color code since the stop's settle ("sync", from dmr_call_last_activity, a late entry still waiting for its LC included) the probe lasts up to 1.5 s: a call already in progress is taken from its embedded LC, which needs the next complete superframe at best and the one after it when the modem's sticky LC equals this call's (about 1.1 s from the retune). Each DMR stop on a new frequency restarts the modem's receiver, so it searches from the retune and not on the last transmitter's slot timing (gotchas 17.2). A call known not to be for us (another talkgroup) ends the probe. P25 and NXDN stops read a carrier as DMR does, probe up to 700 ms for a call of the channel's NAC or RAN, up to 1.5 s while its squelch waits for a field (a late entry's talk group or group), hold on a call that plays and move on from one that doesn't (p25.md 7.1). Digital monitor on when a zone or VFO scan starts (the hear-all latch, dmr-voice.md 1) stays on at every stop (hear_all_latch in the view), so a call to any talkgroup is played and holds the scan; the channel the scan ends on, by STOP, CANCEL or PTT, keeps it latched. Toggling it during the scan turns it on or off for the rest of the scan. Dual watch doesn't carry it. Empty channels still move on after the dwell.

A mixed frequency. A zone may hold an FM channel and digital channels (DMR, P25, NXDN) on one receive frequency. A digital signal can open the FM squelch, and a scan that held the FM stop would sit through the call there and never reach the channel that plays it. So before an FM stop holds, the scan checks the zone's digital channels on that frequency in zone order, each as a stop with its usual probe (scan_mixed in radio_sm.c). A call there holds it. A stop that heard its own mode (a call not for us included) ends the check, and the sweep goes on from where it was. When none heard its mode, the signal was FM: the scan goes back to the FM stop, which holds this time. Trunked channels are not stops (above), so they are never checked. The view counts the stops probed (scan_probes) and those with sync (scan_synced) for each scan. Active: the scan holds (scan_phase=hold), then follows scan_resume; after the signal ends it waits scan_delay_ms (resume) and picks up again if the signal comes back. MONITOR held pauses the scan (paused).

Stopping: STOP stays where the scan is. CANCEL goes back to the starting channel or frequency. PTT on a held signal stops there and talks back on it; PTT while still searching goes back to the start channel and transmits there. A channel, zone, VFO or frequency change by the user ends the scan where the user put it. Nuisance delete skips the current channel (VFO: frequency, up to 8) until the scan ends, and moves on (not in dual watch). A scan starts only when there is another channel to go to; it does not run in hotspot mode or while transmitting.

2.1 Dual watch

On a radio with one receiver, dual watch alternates it between two channels with the scan's timing (a pseudo dual watch). A radio with a second receiver (2.2) hears both at once. radio_req_dual(ref, sel) names the two, A and B: each a channel (zone index and position, any zone) or a VFO with its own settings. ref[sel] is the selected channel. NULL stops the watch on the selected channel. The view shows scan=dual, and the tuned channel as usual: its zone_index, zone_pos and zone name, or vfo=1 and vfo_index, so the screens can tell which of the two the receiver is on.

There are two VFOs, so each channel can have its own: a VFO ref's pos says which (0 or 1). The radio core keeps the one in use in vfo_ch and the other in vfo_other; a hop to a VFO pane swaps them first. Both are saved, with the one in use (settings-store.md 3, second VFO). Outside dual watch the VFO key opens the one last used. Copy to VFO during the watch copies into a VFO the other channel isn't on: the one last used, else the other.

radio_get_dual() returns the pair, the selected one and the one the receiver is on (the debug link's dual.a dual.b dual.sel dual.at, debuglink.md 3.7); the view's dual_busy is dual.busy. radio_get_vfo(index) returns a VFO's settings while something else is tuned (a VFO pane). Built without CONFIG_DUAL_WATCH only the debug link's dual watch is left: two channels of the current zone, and PTT ends it as it ends any scan.

A P25 or NXDN channel (a stored one, or the VFO in that mode) can be either side. The scan's rules apply to it (2): it is one only where the board receives that mode at the channel's rate, never as a trunked channel, and a look there starts its engine, probes a carrier for up to 700 ms and holds for a call that plays. The air interface runs one engine at a time, so with P25 on one side and NXDN on the other each hop stops one engine and starts the other. The UI refuses a trunked channel at once; a mode the board can't receive is refused by the radio core, and the watch doesn't start. The panes name the mode and show no tone or bandwidth for it, and don't show the caller of a P25 or NXDN call.

2.2 A second receiver

A board with a second receiver sets board.rx2 (hal/hal_rx2.h; the GD-88's second SoC, over the link in rx2-link.md). In dual watch the main receiver then stays on the selected channel and the second one gets the other. Nothing alternates, and the scan's hold and resume timing aren't used. It takes FM and DMR only: when the channel not selected is P25 or NXDN, the main receiver alternates as in 2.1. The view's rx2 is 1 while this is so (the debug link's dual.both).

ui_svc_receivers() is 2 on such a radio, and ui_svc_dual_rx(pane) reports each pane's own receiver while the second one has its channel: the dual watch screens then show a call on each pane at once.

3. FM transmit extras

3.1 Busy channel lockout and TX inhibit

tx_check refuses PTT, before keying: TX inhibit (inhibit), a critical battery (battery, 5.2), busy channel lockout (busy; carrier = squelch open or noise below the open threshold; wrong tone = that, while the channel's RX tone is not decoded). The deny beep sounds, except for busy; the state is tx_denied until PTT is released. A busy refusal is quiet so the call being received is heard in full: no deny beep (FM and DMR), and on FM a reception that was playing plays on while PTT is held (deny_rx), until the squelch would have closed. After the release it is an ordinary reception again, its RX tone still counted as heard.

The test TX limit (core/radio/tx_limit.h, built with CONFIG_DEBUGLINK) is a limit a test harness sets over the debug link (RADIO op 35, debuglink.md 3.8): block every transmission, or allow only a list of up to 10 frequencies. It lives in RAM and is never saved, so a restart clears it; the TX inhibit setting is saved and is left alone. The radio core checks it wherever it keys the transmitter (key_tx: PTT, DMR data and replies, roaming probes, APRS beacons, the SFR repeater role, the hotspot, the CW ID), against the frequency it is about to key on. PTT is denied as by the TX inhibit (inhibit); what the radio sends by itself is not keyed and counted (txlim.refused). A transmission already on air when the limit is set goes on until it ends. The HIL harness uses it to keep each test on the rig's transmit frequencies (tests/hil/README.md, "Transmit frequencies").

3.2 Time-out warning

tot_warn_s before the time-out the TOT warning beep sounds once in the speaker (FM and DMR; the view's tot_warning=1). At the time-out the transmitter goes off, tx_deny=timeout until PTT is released.

3.3 Roger beep and 1750 Hz burst (CONFIG_FM_TX_TONES)

Both go out over the air through the board's tx_tone(hz) hook: an audio tone into the modulator in place of the microphone. Roger beep: at PTT release the radio stays keyed for 1200 Hz then 900 Hz, 100 ms each (roger=1), then unkeys; PTT again during the beep cancels it and the transmission goes on. Tone burst: radio_req_tone_burst(true, max_ms) keys up if needed and sends 1750 Hz until false or until max_ms has passed (repeater access); tone_burst=1 in the view. The debug link always gives a limit (at most INPUT_INJECT_MAX_MS), so a lost "off" unkeys as the "off" would; 0 means no limit but the TX time-out. A board without tx_tone gets neither. The debug link's DL_OP_TONE_BURST now gives the error beep when refused on a DMR channel, in hotspot mode or without tx_tone, instead of silently doing nothing. A debug-link burst requested during the roger beep tail after PTT release now re-keys, cancels the roger beep and owns the transmission until burst off, the host deadline or the TX time-out.

The Tone burst side-key press function sends raw down/up requests (radio_req_tone_burst_key). The core starts 1750 Hz on down and holds it for at least RADIO_BURST_MIN_MS (1000 ms). A tap releases at that minimum; a longer hold ends on up. The 10 ms radio tick ends a released timed burst. From receive it keys and unkeys through the usual TX checks (including TX inhibit and RX-only); DMR, hotspot and boards without tx_tone refuse with an error beep. During PTT it replaces the mic, then restores it while PTT keeps the transmitter keyed. Releasing PTT during the burst stops the tone and unkeys, without a roger beep. PTT pressed during a burst takes over the transmission. There is no duration setting or host deadline for the side key; the TX time-out still applies. The debug link's explicit on/off request keeps its max_ms limit. A new burst starts with only its own timing; an on/down request while a burst is already sounding does not restart it or change its deadline. A side-key up releases only its own burst or TX denial; down/up during a host burst leaves that burst running. Power-off cancels a held or timed burst and unkeys without a roger beep before shutdown waits. VOX cannot release during a burst; its full quiet tail starts after the burst ends.

A channel can send the burst by itself (channel_t.tone_burst, the channel editor's Tone burst row; stored in SFR byte B bit 6, open-firmware-cps.md 7.3.3). Each PTT key-up on it opens with one timed burst, as the side key pressed during PTT: 1750 Hz for RADIO_BURST_MIN_MS, then the mic. Only a PTT press that keys the transmitter starts one: not a press during a transmission, not VOX, the side key or the debug link. FM channels only, never the VFO, DMR or hotspot mode, and nothing on a board without tx_tone. A board whose tx_tone refuses the tone gives the error beep and the transmission goes on.

Why 1 s: the UK repeater access standard accepts 1750 Hz +/- 25 Hz after 200-400 ms, and some decoders need 500 ms; a 1 s minimum exceeds both. Once open, a repeater stays open on carrier through its hang time, so one burst opens a contact.

The board restores the microphone path on tx_tone(0) and on every return to receive. An AT1846S board makes the tone with the voice source select (tone 1, tone 2 or both) in place of the microphone. Verified on air on an HR_C6000 + AT1846S board (HIL 2026-10-10): the burst, the roger beep and APRS AFSK (docs/testing/on-air-coverage.md).

3.3a DCS turn-off tail (CONFIG_SUBTONE)

A DCS transmission ends with the turn-off code: 134.4 Hz (the DCS bit rate) as a steady tone in place of the code. Receivers set to the code close their squelch on it while the carrier is still up, so they don't play the noise burst of the carrier dropping.

3.4 VOX (CONFIG_VOX; the vox setting is off by default)

On FM channels (all, or those with the codeplug VOX flag) the microphone level (mic_level(), 0-100 logarithmic) at or above 100 - 8 x vox_level for 2 polls (50 ms) keys up; below it for vox_tail_ms unkeys (with the roger beep if on). Received audio, MONITOR, and 500 ms after a reception never key VOX (the speaker into the microphone). PTT works as usual and wins. The level comes from the board's board.power->mic_level hook; a board without it has no VOX. Boards put their reading on the hal's scale with miclevel_log() in drivers/audio/miclevel/miclevel.h (100 / 12 steps per bit, about 0.72 dB per step), so a threshold 100 - 8 x vox_level sits (8 x vox_level) x 0.72 dB below the top of the scale: about 6 dB at sensitivity 1, 29 dB at 5, 58 dB at 10.

Board kind Where the level comes from Checked
STM32 boards with an analog level tap an ADC channel on the microphone level, 12 bits, passed straight in in silence only (test_vox_quiet)
HR_C7000 boards the codec ADC's mic PCM, 80 samples every 10 ms (platform/hrc7000/audio_pcm.c, drivers/audio/miclevel): the peak of each half from its mean, 100 = a full-scale 16-bit peak; the level follows a louder half at once and falls 4 steps per half. These radios have no analog level tap. The first mic_level() call starts the meter (AUDIO_CONTROL b4 and the PCM interrupt, shared with the PCM stream) and returns 0; 1 s without a call stops it. The meter's interrupt clears b4 itself, so every AUDIO_CONTROL update in the driver (FM receive, transmit and idle, DMR entry) runs with interrupts off (hrc7000_set_crit, set by hrc_audio_pcm_attach). Needs CONFIG_DMR (the PCM stream); without it there is no hook not on the bench

The debug link's vox.level (features section) calls the hook on FM channels outside hotspot mode, so the level can be read with VOX off.

3.5 Squelch tail elimination

No reverse burst or tone-loss signal from the other station is acted on yet; closing early on CTCSS loss conflicts with the tone debounce (8 polls) that keeps fades from chopping audio.

The noise squelch shortens the tail itself. It closes after RADIO_SQL_CLOSE_POLLS (2) polls over the close line (open threshold + RADIO_SQL_HYST_DB), 50 ms, but after one poll when the noise is RADIO_SQL_FAST_CLOSE_DB (12 dB) or more over it: a carrier that goes takes the noise far over the line at once, while a fade crosses it by a little. Squelch level 0 (always open) and the APRS hold (RADIO_SQL_CLOSE_POLLS_APRS) keep their own timing. On one board (BK4819 receiver) the tail at the speaker went from 55 ms to 15-35 ms in three calls (measured, test_fm_rx_speaker); what is left is the poll and the chip's noise reading lag.

A key map entry names its digit (keymap_entry_t.dtmf); a digit key's is its own digit. A microphone with extra keys (P4-P7) sends A-D. keep their usual meaning.

Synthesis: a 64-entry sine table with linear interpolation, 2 ms fades (no clicks); level 100 is about half of full scale so a beep mixed over voice doesn't clip. Beeps follow the radio's volume up to beep_level. A radio with an analog knob (no hal_radio_t.volume) and one whose volume hook also scales the PCM path (volume_pcm: an HR_C6000 board's RX gain, an HR_C7000 board's line out gain) play the beep at beep_level, and the volume acts on it in the speaker path. On a radio where the MCU sets the volume somewhere the PCM path doesn't pass, the core plays each beep at the volume percent last given to the board, capped at beep_level (radio_beep_level): volume 0 gives no beep. When the DMR voice stream runs, the beep is mixed into it (ambeVoiceSetMixer). A DMR call leaves that stream running until the radio leaves DMR, so most beeps on a DMR channel are mixed. A mixed beep the amplifier isn't ready for (more than one lead-in block, below) gets the same lead-in as silence mixed in with the speaker routed; otherwise its tone starts in the first block. Without the voice stream the beep starts the board's PCM audio stream itself, routes the speaker for silent lead-in blocks before the tone (one 20 ms block, or hal_audio_t.speaker_lead_ms rounded up to blocks, at most 200 ms, for an amplifier that unmutes slowly), holds the route for four silent blocks after the pattern, and then stops the stream. A beep in that hold plays at once, with no lead-in. A board with speaker_hang_s (a mobile on a supply; 0 on battery radios) keeps its amplifier powered and silent (speaker_warm) from any key, PTT, reception or beep until that many seconds after the last one, so a key click or the DMR talk permit tone plays at once: while it is warm, beeps take the one-block lead-in. If that stream delivers no block for 200 ms (8 radio polls), its clock has stopped: the beep stops the stream, gives the speaker route back and drops the pattern, so a codec that goes quiet can't leave the analog receive audio muted. A start that fails (no codec clock) is stopped the same way, in case a block already ran. Counters in the view: beep.played (started), beep.no_stream (no audio stream could be started: on a board that can't stream PCM in FM mode this counts instead), beep.blocks (audio blocks with beep in them), beep.stalls (streams given up that way), beep.busy; in the audio section, beep.shared (patterns mixed into the voice stream), beep.leads (lead-in blocks played), beep.warm (1: the amplifier is held warm) and beep.warms (times it was powered up for that).

To hear the beeps in context, tools/beep_reel.py makes a video of the UI simulator's screen in which every beep sounds in a scene that sounds it on a radio, captioned (build/beep-reel/beep_reel.mp4; needs ffmpeg built with libass). It uses beep.c only through beep_init, beep_play and beep_service, so the patterns and their synthesis can change freely. The scenes are in tests/host/beepReel.c; a new beep needs one there, or the host tests fail. A pull request that changes the beeps gets the reel before and after (beep-reel.yml, ci.md "Beep reel").

4.3 Signaling tones (CONFIG_SIG_TONES)

Short tone sequences at three events, on the speaker, on air or both, set separately for DMR and for FM (core/radio/sigtone.h, Settings > Sound). Each sequence is up to 10 steps of {Hz, ms} (SIG_STEPS), at most 900 ms in all (SIG_MAX_MS), a pause being a step of 0 Hz: the shape of the rows of a CPS alert tone page.

Event Where it fires Default (the output setting 0)
start (SIG_EV_BEGIN) DMR: the talk permit's place. FM: when the transmitter keys DMR: the talk permit (talk_permit). FM: none
end (SIG_EV_END) DMR: our transmission is over. FM: PTT released DMR: none. FM: the roger beep, if roger_beep is on
idle (SIG_EV_IDLE) DMR: a call heard ended. FM: the squelch closed after a reception DMR: the call end beep. FM: none

Output (sig_out_t): default leaves the row above as it was and an older settings record reads it, off silences the event (the talk permit, the call end beep and the roger beep included), speaker, on air and speaker + air replace it with the sequence. The idle event has no on air copy: clamp_settings maps it to what the speaker can do. The FM start tone on air plays right after the transmitter keys (fm_lead_start, on the roger_seg chain through tx_tone) and the microphone goes live when it ends; releasing the PTT during it cancels it. A board without tx_tone sends none. A channel with "no beep" has none of the speaker or on air copies. The tones on the speaker are the BEEP_CUSTOM pattern of beep.c (beep_play_custom: two copies of the steps alternate, so a pattern still playing is not rewritten), and a default output keeps the fixed beeps and their Settings > Sound switches (the FM roger beep has none: roger_beep is set over the debug link). The sound is the event's chain (sig_chain): up to SIG_STEPS (10) steps, each a frequency (0 = a pause, up to SIG_HZ_MAX, 3400 Hz, a multiple of SIG_HZ_UNIT, 10 Hz) and a length (multiples of 10 ms), the same shape as the Frequency / Period rows of a CPS alert tone page. A step of length 0 ends the chain, and the whole chain is cut to SIG_MAX_MS (900 ms: on air the call stack holds the over open for three superframes, about 1.08 s). A chain with no step is the event's standard sound (the permit beep at the start, the call end beep otherwise). Settings > Signaling tones edits the three chains in settings_t (the sig-chain screen, ui.md 2): nothing is built in beyond that standard sound.

The three events are also keys of the codeplug's settings area (0x0007-0x0009, codeplug-settings.md 3.3): the outputs and the chain of an event, as one record. A key that is present holds over the radio's own copy, and a change from the menu writes the event's record (one write for a step's frequency and length) as every keyed setting does (5.2). A record with a value the setting can't take reads as absent. Without CONFIG_SIG_TONES the keys are kept and carried but do nothing.

On air How
DMR start ambeVoiceTxTone is asked for as PTT goes down; the encoder sends the sequence in place of the microphone from the transmission's first block, after the headers. The encoder waits for room in its queue (AMBE_VOICE_QUEUE, 8 frames) before each block of the tone, so none of it is lost to an overflow
DMR end asked for as PTT is let go while on air, with the microphone muted after it (final). The call stack holds the over open while the source is busy (dmr_voice_source_t.busy: the tone not yet pulled), one superframe at a time, at most DMR_CALL_TX_TAIL_HOLDS (3, about 1.1 s). A time-out ends the over at once
FM end roger_poll steps through the sequence with hal_radio_t.tx_tone, from each step's own start (a step begins at the first poll, RADIO_POLL_MS, after its time), then unkeys. The default is the two step roger beep. A board with no tx_tone sends none

The tone is encoded as a tone frame (VOCODER_OPT_TONE_FRAMES) with the noise reduction off while it plays, and the options are put back after it. AMBE is a speech codec: a steady tone passes, a sequence of steps shorter than about 60 ms is two or three frames each and may sound rough, and what another make's radio does with a tone frame is not specified here. A start tone is sent with the host's audio (USRP) never, and a start tone longer than the encoder queue can hold waits for the call stack, which delays the voice that much. The tone counts in the TX time-out.

The DMR on air copy carries SIG_AIR_GUARD_MS (60 ms) of silence on its outer side before a start tone and SIG_AIR_END_GUARD_MS (100 ms) after an end tone, which a receiver slow to close its audio cut now and then at 60. A link that drops the first or last frames of a transmission (a hotspot, a repeater that opens late) then loses the silence and not the tone. The guard counts in the tone's length for the tail hold: 900 ms and the longer guard (1000 ms) are inside the three superframes (1080 ms) the over is held for.

On the speaker the start tone follows the board: a board with CAP_BEEP_NOT_ON_AIR plays a DMR start tone before keying, in the talk permit's hold (permit_before_key), and plays the FM start tone on the speaker as it keys (only the DMR engine closes the audio path). A board with hal_radio_t.tx_tone_side (the modem's sidetone) hears an FM start tone set to speaker + air from the tone sent on air: the core turns the sidetone on with the tone and off when it ends, and plays no separate beep. The end tone plays once the transmitter is off, and on a board whose audio path is closed while the DMR engine sends its last burst (hal_audio_t.speaker_blocked) a little after that: audio queued while the path is closed is dropped when it opens, so the core waits for it, at most DMR_END_WAIT_MS (400 ms), and then plays it whole.

5. Power

5.1 Battery

The radio core reads battery_mv every second (not while transmitting nor for 3 s after: the sag under TX is not the battery's state) and shows battery_mv, battery_pct (the board's) and battery (ok / low / critical). Thresholds are per board (board.battery_low_mv, battery_critical_mv; 0 means no warnings; values in each radio's hardware page). Three readings in a row below a threshold lower the level; it goes back up 200 mV above it.

A board with CAP_NO_BATTERY (a mobile on a fixed supply) has no battery monitor. The core never reads battery_mv, the level stays ok, battery_pct and battery_mv stay 0, and nothing warns or refuses a transmission for the battery. The UI shows no battery icon, percentage, Tools > Battery page, Battery saver setting or Low battery warning setting. The debug link reports battery=none (with battery_pct=0 and battery_mv=0), /status leaves battery_pct and battery_mv out, and SNMP reports rfoundryBatteryVoltage 0, rfoundryBatteryPercent 0 and rfoundryBatteryState ok(1) without ever sending rfoundryLowBattery.

Such a board may measure its DC input instead: it sets hal_power_t.supply_mv, and board_volts() (hal/board.h) says BOARD_VOLTS_SUPPLY. The radio core reads it once a second, outside transmissions like the battery, into the view's supply_mv. Nothing acts on it: no level, percentage, warning, TX refusal or power off, since no supply thresholds are defined yet. The UI's last tool is then Supply, showing the reading in volts (13.8 V), with no battery settings. The debug link has supply_mv in place of battery_pct and battery_mv, /status has supply_mv, and SNMP reports as for no battery. The feature matrix and the web site show each radio's reading: Battery, Supply or None.

5.2 Low and critical

Low: the battery low beep, again every 2 minutes; the backlight is capped at 30 % (5.3). Critical: the critical beep every 30 s, every transmission refused (tx_deny=battery), and with battery_off_s set the radio core asks for power off after that long (power_off=1, radio_power_off_wanted()); core/app/app.c then powers off as with the switch.

5.3 Backlight

radio_backlight_pct(brightness, idle_ms): the UI's brightness, dimmed to a fifth (at least 5 %) after backlight_dim_s without input, off after backlight_off_s, capped at 30 % while the battery is low or critical. The UI owns the backlight and calls it; the radio core doesn't drive the backlight itself.

Either time 0 means never: backlight_dim_s 0 never dims, backlight_off_s 0 never turns the backlight off, so a dim time with off 0 dims and stays on. The UI's setting (UI_SET_BACKLIGHT_S, Settings > Display > Backlight Dim/Off) carries both in one value: a time T > 0 dims after T and goes off after 4T, 0 is always on, and -T dims after T and never goes off (the "T/dim" choices).

Idle time restarts on input and while radio_view_wakes(view) holds: something the user would want to see.

State Wakes Why
RS_TX, tx_auto 0 yes the user's PTT (or VOX)
RS_TX, tx_auto 1 no the radio's own: a data list (ARS, ACK, LRRP answer, IP), a roaming probe, an APRS beacon
RS_TX_DENIED yes PTT held: the screen says why
RS_RX, FM yes the squelch opened: something plays
RS_RX, DMR, dmr.state RDMR_RX yes a call that plays
RS_RX, DMR, otherwise no data heard (ARS ACK, others' CSBKs), a call not played (other talkgroup, encrypted)
RS_RX, bcast no a broadcast program plays continuously
hotspot no the host's traffic

What does reach the user (a message, call alert, private call, radio check result, emergency) comes as a UI banner or screen, and the UI wakes the backlight for it (ui_app_take_wake). last_tx_ms in the view records when the last transmission began, so an unattended one can be placed afterwards (debuglink.md 3.7).

5.4 Receiver battery save (CONFIG_BATTERY_SAVE, rx_save off by default)

On an idle FM channel after rx_save_idle_s without activity (keys, requests, receptions, PTT), the receiver sleeps 200 / 400 / 800 ms and listens 100 ms (rx_sleeping in the view). Any signal in the listen window keeps it awake; anything else (a key, a request, MONITOR, scan, VOX, DMR channel, hotspot, a channel with the codeplug "no eco" flag, APRS receive listening on the APRS frequency, aprs.md 5.3) stops it. The board's rx_sleep hook turns the receiver off and back on. A board that sets rx_sleep_dmr also lets a trunked system's power save use it on a DMR channel (dmr-trunking.md 7.13). On air on an AT1846S board since 2026-10-10 (test_battery_save*: cycles when idle, MONITOR and a call wake it); the setting stays off by default.

5.5 Power switch

On boards with a volume knob that has a switch, the switch doesn't cut the supply: the MCU holds its own with the power latch, and the switch only breaks the battery sense line, so the unfiltered battery reading drops to about 0 V the moment the knob turns off. core/radio/power_switch.c turns that into the board's switch_on(): three readings in a row below 5.5 V, at least 10 ms apart, mean off; one low reading doesn't. The boards ignore it for the first 1.5 s after power-up while the readings settle. core/app/app.c polls switch_on() and powers off (settings saved, latch released). 5.5 V sits well under a flat 2S pack (critical 6.0 V), so a weak battery never reads as the switch.

Boards with a power key instead (mobiles) feed the key's level to core/radio/power_key.c from their switch_on(). The press that switched the radio on is usually still held at start-up, so the key counts only once it has been seen released; then 20 polls held in a row (2 s at the 100 ms poll) mean off. A shorter press doesn't count toward the next.

5.6 Time of day

RadioFoundry keeps no wall clock. hal_time.h gives only time since start-up, and that is what every record uses: the crash log's uptime_ms, messages (ui_msg_t.age_ms), call history and the APRS station list ("14m ago"). Nothing on the screen shows a time of day, so a radio never shows a made-up one after power-on.

The only time of day is the UTC time a GPS or host NMEA fix carries. It travels with that position into DMR GPS and LRRP reports. A fixed position carries none, and the reports leave the time out (utc_hms 0xFFFFFFFF, date 0).

Some radios have a real-time clock, and some keep it running through power-off. board.toml [hardware] clock records which, with its source (backed, unbacked, none, or unknown), and the feature matrix and the site show it. A clock driver arrives behind a hardware switch (CONFIG_RTC, hardware in tests/hil/features.toml). Until GPS, the network or the user sets it, the radio shows "no time", and records keep using uptime or mark the time unknown.

5.7 LEDs

The radio core drives the board's red and green LEDs (board.power->led); view.leds (debug link leds) is what it drives. Red: transmitting. Green: a reception that plays (FM squelch open, a DMR call played), but not a broadcast station. Two timed signals go over that (radio_sm.c led_signals, the times in radio_sm.h), for radios without a screen above all:

Signal LEDs
Waking a repeater (DMR, PTT through a repeater that isn't up) red once per wake-up burst sent, RADIO_LED_WAKE_MS (100 ms) each, dark while the radio listens for the answer. The call stack counts the bursts (dmr_call_view_t.wake_sent)
A TX deny, every reason (RS_TX_DENIED), and a repeater that didn't wake after a short press was let go red, green, red, green, red, green, RADIO_LED_DENY_STEP_MS (100 ms) each, once, with the deny tone (none for a busy channel, 3.1); then what the state shows. A transmission that starts during it ends it
The PTT line stuck low, which only a power cycle releases (gotchas.md 16.5) red and green in turn, 125 ms each, until the power cycle

Once the repeater answers and the voice goes out, red is solid as for any transmission. The tick and the poll keep calling outputs() while a signal runs, so it keeps time when nothing else changes.

6. TX power, calibration, band limits

Checked, unchanged: the board clamps the power level, uses the calibration table's drive value for the band and level, and refuses a TX frequency outside the board's bands (tx_deny=band). Without a calibration the board either drives a low fixed value or refuses to transmit; the board's hardware page says which. While a hardware option is pending (board_hwopt_pending, hal/hal_hwopt.h: unset, or stored and waiting for a restart) the core refuses PTT itself with tx_deny=hwopt (the screen names the option), before the board is asked: a board may still key while a restart is due. When the board's tx refuses a frequency inside its bands, the reason is tx_deny=hwopt while an option is pending, else tx_deny=refused ("Radio refused to send"); never band.

6.1 Reference trim

Settings > Advanced > Freq trim: a fraction of the reference, -10.0 to +10.0 ppm in 0.1 ppm steps, 0 by default (core/radio/ref_trim.c). It is for a radio whose reference oscillator is off even with its calibration: it moves receive and transmit together, FM and DMR. A fraction of the reference moves every carrier by the same fraction, so one value holds on every band; the Hz it comes to grows with the frequency.

Trim At 440 MHz At 144 MHz
0.1 ppm (one step) 44 Hz 14.4 Hz
4.5 ppm 1980 Hz 648 Hz
10.0 ppm (the limit) 4.4 kHz 1.44 kHz

The value is held in parts per billion (ppb, 100 a step), an int16: hal_radio_t.ref_trim, settings_t.ref_trim_ppb, the debug link. The UI stores it and applies it at start-up and on each change with radio_set_ref_trim(), which clamps, rounds to the step and calls hal_radio_t.ref_trim when the value differs from the one the board has (the board starts at 0, so a start-up with no trim calls nothing). The debug link's SETTING op 35 sets it in RAM only.

Board Trim
with a reference control (hal_radio_t.ref_trim sets it) adds the trim to the control its calibration sets, at every tune; its hardware page gives the scale
with synthesizers only (hal_radio_t.ref_trim keeps the value) programs each RF chip at f x (1 + trim) instead of f, at every tune and burst (ref_trim_tune_hz). A superhet's LO takes f x (1 + trim) + IF and its IF stays as it is: both chips run from one reference, so the IF's share of the error cancels
without the hook, or built with CONFIG_REF_TRIM=n the row is hidden; nothing changes

The hook returns whether the board takes the trim at the next tune. A board that applies it at once returns false; one that applies it when it tunes returns true, and the caller (the UI, the debug link) then asks the radio task to tune again (radio_req_retune, the RREQ_RETUNE request). A tune ends a reception in progress, so the radio task tunes once the trim has held still RADIO_RETUNE_SETTLE_MS (400 ms), not at each step: when idle, after an APRS transmission, or at the end of a transmission or the hotspot. Until then the hotspot or a transmission in progress keeps the trim it started with on receive. The RF chips' bus stays the radio task's.

The value is a correction to enter by hand: measure the carrier against a reference (a frequency counter, a service monitor, or a hotspot's MMDVMCal BER test) and enter the error with its sign reversed. A radio 2 kHz high at 448.7 MHz is 4.5 ppm high and takes -4.5 ppm. feature-matrix.md lists which radios have the hook.

6.2 Receive past the band table (CONFIG_RX_EXT)

A board may list receive-only ranges past its rated bands: the last board_t.rx_ext_count entries of bands, with tx false. They count only while *board_t.rx_ext_on is set.

Rule Detail
Setting Settings > Advanced > RX past bands, off by default. Stored in settings_t.cp_flags, cleared by a factory reset. Hidden on a board with no range.
Table Everything that reads the bands uses board_band_count(): the VFO's tuning and wrap, the keypad entry, VFO scan, the channel editor, rigctld and the debug link's bands=.
Transmit Never: board_in_band(hz, true) skips the entries, so PTT gives TXDENY_BAND.
Modes FM only, as in any receive-only band: the channel editor refuses DMR there.
Turning it off The radio core reloads. A VFO past the table returns to a band; a channel there stays stored and doesn't tune.

A board's ranges come from its receiver chip's documents. Nothing past the rated bands is measured: front-end filters may cut sensitivity well inside the chip's range, and spurs aren't specified. A radio's ranges are on its hardware page.
