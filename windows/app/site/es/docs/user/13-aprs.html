APRS

APRS (Automatic Packet Reporting System) shares your position with other amateur stations and maps such as aprs.fi. The radio sends APRS two ways:

  • On FM: as 1200 baud AFSK packets on the APRS frequency of your region. The radio leaves your channel for about a second, sends, and comes back.
  • Over DMR: as a position report to an APRS gateway ID (BrandMeister's 310999 by default; it works from any master, but your own master's MCC plus 999 is recommended, for example 204999). This works on DMR channels. BrandMeister forwards Motorola (LRRP) and Hytera position reports to APRS, so keep the default LRRP format (not UDT). A radio that already saved APRS settings keeps its stored ID: run aprs set dmr_dst 310999 (US) or aprs set dmr_dst <MCC>999. The position appears on APRS-IS as your callsign with the SSID from your BrandMeister profile (aprs.fi may take a minute to show it).

Not every radio does both. Some send only, some can't send APRS on FM yet, and a build can leave APRS out for flash room. The Radio reference lists them and says where APRS is verified.

Before you start

  • You need an amateur radio license. APRS sends only on amateur frequencies, and the radio refuses others.
  • Your callsign is the first word of the radio name (Settings > My radio, or the codeplug's radio name). Without a valid callsign nothing is sent, and the APRS page says why.
  • Sending is off until you turn it on. "Send position now" works at any time.

The APRS page

Press OK, 6 (Tools), 8 (APRS). The top of the page shows your callsign, the APRS frequency, your position and its source, and when the next beacon goes out. If the radio can't send, it says why.

Row What it does
Send position now One position at once
Automatic beacons On: positions go out by themselves (see Triggers)
Frequency Your region: N America 144.390, Europe 144.800, Australia 145.175, New Zealand 144.575, Japan 144.660, China 144.640 MHz, or Custom (tools/rfoundry_debug.py aprs set region custom, then aprs set freq_hz 144390000)
Power As channel: APRS transmits at the radio's current power level. Or pick a level of its own, for example a low one for a nearby digipeater. Dimmed on radios that can't send APRS over the air
Path How far digipeaters pass your packets on: None, WIDE1-1, WIDE1-1,WIDE2-1 (the default), WIDE2-1 or WIDE2-2. See Path below. Dimmed on radios that can't send APRS over the air
Interval How often: Off, 1, 2, 5, 10 or 30 minutes
SmartBeaconing More often when you move fast, at once after a turn, rarely when you stand still
Send via AFSK (on FM), DMR, or both
KISS on USB The radio works as a KISS TNC for APRS programs on the computer
Stations heard Stations the radio heard (radios that can receive APRS), newest first. A station that sent its position shows how far away and which way it was from yours, when the radio has a position too; the age on that row is then the age of that position. Other rows show when the station was last heard. Objects and items a station sends don't count as its position, and a position more than a day old is dropped. Select one and choose Message to fill its callsign
SSID The number after your callsign (N0ABC-9): 0 to 15, 7 by default. OK steps up, holding OK steps down; 0 sends the callsign alone. See SSID below. Dimmed on radios that can't send APRS over the air

A firmware built with only one way to send APRS shows only the rows for it. With APRS over DMR only there is no Frequency, Power, Path, Send via, Stations heard or SSID, and KISS on USB is called NMEA on USB (the computer can still give the radio its position).

The symbol, comment and the DMR destination are set with the debug tool for now (tools/rfoundry_debug.py aprs set ...), later with qdmr.

Station details

Open Stations heard, select a callsign, and press OK. Message is first; press OK again to open a message addressed to that station. The selection stays on the same callsign when incoming packets reorder the heard list.

Scroll with Up / Down to see distance and direction (when both positions are known), when the station was heard, Direct or Via digipeater, and the position's age if it differs from the last packet's age. Speed in km/h and course appear only when known for the retained position. The remaining rows show the symbol's table and code, the retained comment or status, and the packet count. The radio retains at most 23 text characters plus the end marker; longer comments are incomplete. No digipeater name is retained.

If the station leaves the heard list, the page says No longer in list. You can still choose Message to address that callsign.

Path

The path says how many digipeater hops a packet may take on FM. Each WIDEn-N entry allows N more hops. Press OK on Path to step to the next choice.

Path Hops Use it
None 0 Only stations that hear you directly (an iGate nearby)
WIDE1-1 1 A fill-in digipeater or one wide digipeater
WIDE1-1,WIDE2-1 2 Mobile and handheld use in most of North America and Europe (the default)
WIDE2-1 1 Fixed stations that reach a wide digipeater directly
WIDE2-2 2 Sparse areas; skips fill-in digipeaters

More hops mean more copies on a busy frequency. Use the fewest that reach an iGate. The path doesn't apply to positions sent over DMR.

The path is also in the codeplug's first APRS configuration, which qdmr edits. A change on the radio is written there too, and a codeplug written with a different path changes the radio's. Whichever changed last is used.

SSID

The SSID tells your stations apart on APRS: a mobile and a handheld with the same callsign need different ones (commonly 9 for a mobile, 7 for a handheld). It applies to packets sent on FM; positions sent over DMR carry the SSID set for your ID at BrandMeister.

Like the path, the SSID is also in the codeplug's first APRS configuration. Set it on the APRS page or in the codeplug: a change on the radio is written to the codeplug, and a codeplug written with a different SSID changes the radio's. Whichever changed last is used. Only the first APRS configuration counts. After a firmware update, a codeplug SSID of 0 is not taken the first time: the radio keeps its own (7 by default). To send without an SSID, set 0 on the radio.

Receiving

On radios that can receive APRS, the radio decodes packets while you listen on the APRS frequency. Make a channel on the frequency shown in Frequency on the APRS page (your region's, 144.390 MHz in North America) in FM, or tune the VFO there, and leave the radio on it. If the packets are sent on another frequency, set Frequency to Custom and enter it first. Heard stations go into Stations heard, messages to your callsign appear in Messages and are acknowledged, and the digipeater (if you turned it on) passes packets on. Messages from internet stations (an APRS messaging service, for example) reach you through a nearby iGate. They appear under the sender's callsign, and the acknowledgment goes back to that sender.

  • It listens only on the APRS frequency, in FM, and the tuned frequency must equal the APRS Frequency exactly. On other FM channels nothing is decoded.
  • The volume knob doesn't matter: a radio turned all the way down still decodes.
  • On a radio that decodes only what opens the squelch (Radio reference), a packet must be strong enough to open it. Keep the squelch at its usual level, not tight.
  • Battery saving pauses while the radio listens for APRS: a sleeping receiver misses packets.
  • Receiving is on by default. Turn it off with tools/rfoundry_debug.py aprs set rx 0 (FM only).

On a DMR channel, a DMR APRS position another radio sends (some makes send it as an NMEA position) also goes into Stations heard, whoever it is addressed to: usually the APRS gateway, as on FM every station heard is listed. It is listed under the sender's callsign when its DMR ID is in the radio's DMR ID database, else under the DMR ID. This works whatever the receive setting. Stations heard shows the station and when it was heard, not its position.

Triggers

Automatic beacons go out at the interval, or with SmartBeaconing by speed and turns. Never more often than every 30 seconds. The radio waits for a clear APRS frequency before it sends, and gives up after 10 seconds. Pressing PTT always wins: an APRS transmission in progress stops and your channel comes back.

An FM beacon can interrupt dual watch. It always sends on the APRS frequency you set, then dual watch resumes on the channel it was listening to. A or B selection still controls where PTT transmits; it does not choose the beacon frequency. A zone or VFO scan delays the beacon until the scan stops.

Position

The position comes from:

  • GPS: on radios that have one (Radio reference).
  • Computer: NMEA from a GPS on the computer, sent over USB by tools/aprs_gate.py --nmea <GPS port> (KISS on USB must be on).
  • Fixed: a position you enter.

Auto takes a fresh GPS position, else the computer's, else the fixed one.

The position is not only APRS's: DMR calls and location requests (LRRP) use the same one. Its settings are under Settings > Location: Position source (Auto, GPS, Computer, Fixed) and Fixed position, a page with Latitude and Longitude. To set the fixed position:

  1. Open Settings > Location > Fixed position, select Latitude and press OK. The value opens for editing.
  2. Type the degrees with the digits, decimal places included: 3,3,7,4,9,0 is 33.7490. The decimal point is already there. Longitude has three places before the point: 84.3880 is 8,4,3,8,8,0 (the radio puts the leading 0 in for you), and 122.4194 is 1,2,2,4,1,9,4. For 10 to 19 degrees, type the 0 yourself.
  3. Press Up or Down (or turn the knob) to switch between N and S, or E and W. West longitudes are W.
  4. Press OK to save. Places you didn't type count as zeros. BACK removes the last digit; with nothing typed it closes the value unchanged.

Four decimal places put you within about 11 m. You can also type "latitude" or "fixed" on Home to find the page.

GPS status

Tools > Position shows what the radio's GPS is doing, on four pages. Press Up or Down (or turn the knob) for the next page, or press 1 to 4. On a radio with a tall screen, Status also shows the grid locator and degrees, minutes and seconds, so there are three pages.

Page What it shows
1 Status 3D Fix, 2D Fix (Fix when the receiver doesn't say which) or Searching; satellites used and seen; position, altitude, speed, HDOP (accuracy: good, fair, poor) and the UTC time of the fix
2 Sky where each satellite is: the outer ring is the horizon, the middle ring 45 degrees up, north at the top. A filled dot is used in the fix. The colors and the legend show the system: GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou, QZSS
3 Signal each satellite's signal strength as a bar; filled bars are used in the fix
4 Grid your Maidenhead grid locator (FN42li), and the position in degrees, minutes and seconds

"No GPS, or it is off" means the radio has no GPS or Settings > Location > GPS is off. A first fix outdoors takes from a few seconds to a few minutes; indoors there may be none.

With a computer (KISS and iGate)

Turn on KISS on USB, then run tools/aprs_gate.py --port <radio port>:

  • --kiss-tcp 8001: APRS programs (APRSdroid, Xastir, YAAC) connect to TCP port 8001 and send through the radio. The radio sends only packets from your own callsign, at most one every 10 seconds after a burst of three.
  • --call <your call>: connects to APRS-IS. A radio that can receive APRS is then an iGate. It passes what it hears to APRS-IS. If you allow it, it also passes messages from APRS-IS to local stations back on the air.
  • In USB network mode use --port udp:<radio address>.

The radio can't be in hotspot mode at the same time.

In USB network mode the radio does both without the helper. APRS programs connect to the radio's address on TCP port 8001 directly (Services on the radio). The radio logs in to an APRS-IS server itself (APRS-IS from the radio). KISS on USB must be on for both.
